AMA Solutions — Corporate Brand System Handoff

Version: 1.0 · Prepared: 29 October 2026
Purpose: Master brief for Claude, Claude Cowork, Claude Design, Codex, and human designers to develop a complete, usable AMA Solutions corporate identity and brand application system.

1. The assignment

Build AMA Solutions as a coherent, credible corporation with the design depth to support every department and every stage of a client relationship. The output is not a logo exploration. The approved identity is the starting point for a complete operating brand: strategy, visual standards, templates, digital components, document families, and implementation-ready assets.

The brand must work for an emerging AI-enabled services corporation while feeling established, calm, accountable, and human. It should be suitable for a serious executive briefing, an invoice, a contract, a website, a digital product, and eventually a physical emblem or lapel pin.

The system should communicate:

Strategic intelligence made practical.

A coordinated team of digital workers supporting capable people.

Human accountability, clear ownership, and professional delivery.

Ambition at scale, expressed with restraint.

A premium, modern identity that does not look like a generic AI startup, crypto token, game guild, or luxury hotel.

2. Brand facts and vocabulary

Public-facing company name: AMA Solutions. Use the legal entity name only where needed for contracts, invoices, tax, banking, and legal notices; confirm the exact suffix from corporate records before production.

Company role: Strategic advisory and AI-enabled services corporation. Describe only the services, teams, and integrations that are actually available.

Core ambition: Build an effective human-and-digital workforce that augments the capabilities of exceptional people. The 144-worker idea is a founding symbol and design principle; it is not a claim that 144 workers are currently deployed.

Architecture context: The wider ecosystem includes AMA, Hanzo, and Lux, coordinated through HAMAL. Keep AMA’s identity independent and client-facing. Do not present partner brands as AMA departments or imply a partner commitment without approval.

Preferred language: “digital workers,” “human-led,” “AI-enabled,” “strategic advisory,” “clear accountability,” “designed capacity,” “in development,” and “available now” when accurate.

Avoid promising autonomous outcomes, guaranteed results, a fully staffed 144-agent workforce, or capabilities that have not been verified live.

3. Approved identity foundation

Approved mark

Use the approved AMA badge/emblem shown in the supplied reference asset: a dimensional gold circular medallion, a sculptural AMA monogram, and small gold head/node forms within the letterforms. The badge is the de facto logo approved by the founder. The standalone monogram is the same identity without the outer badge.

The small heads/nodes represent the collective digital workforce. Preserve their balanced, evenly distributed visual rhythm. The mark should retain the feeling of an elegant metal emblem: precise, dimensional, warm, and restrained.

Do not:

Replace the approved mark with a new concept or a generic AI symbol.

Change the monogram’s structure, add another ring, or simplify the node pattern without explicit founder approval.

Make the heads look like a crowd illustration, cartoon faces, or decorative confetti.

imply a literal verified headcount from the mark.

Recreate a 3D logo from scratch when the approved source asset is available. Use clean vector artwork for small-scale and print production, retaining the approved geometry.

Brand character

Elegant · intelligent · warm · precise · modern · quietly ambitious · accountable.

The identity should feel premium through material, spacing, typography, and craft—not through excess ornament, oversized claims, or gold applied to every surface.

Visual balance

Primary expression: near-black/charcoal, warm metallic gold, generous negative space.

Use the emblem as a seal or signature, not wallpaper.

The dimensional mark may appear on brand covers and hero moments; use a flat, legible master for forms, small screens, embossing, and monochrome reproduction.

Favor subtle material cues—brushed gold, soft metallic highlights, fine rules, calm gradients—over bright chrome, lens flares, glowing circuitry, or busy particle effects.

5. Typography and layout

Select a type system with clear hierarchy, broad language support, and dependable licensing. Show actual font names and licensing/source in the design kit before handoff.

Direction:

Display: refined contemporary serif or sculpted humanist face for high-level statements and document titles. Keep it highly legible; avoid ornate fashion-editorial styling.

Body: neutral, highly readable sans serif for website, forms, contracts, and operational documents.

Data / labels: restrained mono or tabular sans for IDs, dates, status, and technical metadata.

Use sentence case for most headings; reserve all caps for short labels and formal document metadata.

Use generous margins, strong alignment, predictable grids, and visible whitespace.

Contracts and invoices prioritize clarity and legibility over decorative styling.

Deliver a typography specimen with heading levels, body sizes, labels, tables, footnotes, digital scaling, and print examples.

6. Graphic language and imagery

Graphic elements

Circular badge geometry, fine concentric rules, subtle 12-part or 12×12-inspired grid references, and node motifs may support layouts when used sparingly.

Geometry must be abstract and secondary. Do not claim that decorative dots are a precise map of the deployed workforce.

Use thin gold rules, structured columns, modular panels, quiet section numbering, and elegant document folios.

Use diagrams to clarify a real process, responsibility, or service—not as decorative “AI network” art.

Photography and renders

Favor authentic people, real work environments, architecture, materials, and understated editorial photography.

Show human judgment alongside software and digital workers; avoid robot stock imagery, humanoid robot heads, glowing brains, random code, and generic blue-purple AI gradients.

The badge may be rendered as an actual metal object for campaign/brand imagery, but do not distort the approved emblem.

Use image generation for mood exploration only. Final corporate assets must be selected, reviewed, licensed, and checked for factual fidelity.

Motion

Motion should feel deliberate: a subtle turn of light across the medallion, a slow reveal of the nodes, or a clean transition from emblem to system diagram.

Respect reduced-motion preferences. No flashing, rapid particle swarms, or movement that interferes with reading.

7. Voice and messaging system

Voice qualities

Direct, strategic, composed, and warm.

Explain complex systems in plain language.

Make clear who is responsible, what happens next, and what is still under development.

Confident without hype. Ambitious without implying guaranteed results.

Messaging hierarchy

Company: AMA Solutions is a human-led, AI-enabled strategic services corporation.

Value: We help organizations make complex opportunities actionable through strategy, advisory, and well-scoped AI-enabled work.

Operating principle: Digital workers extend the reach of capable people; accountable humans remain responsible for decisions and delivery.

Scale vision: The 144-worker system is an ambition/design framework. Clearly label any part that is planned, prototyped, in development, or verified live.

Copy rules

State the audience, problem, action, and deliverable concretely.

Never invent client names, testimonials, certifications, pricing, performance claims, staffing levels, or partner endorsements.

Label proposed services and sample content as examples.

Distinguish an inquiry, a proposal, an accepted contract, a paid order, and completed delivery.

Use accessible language and avoid unexplained acronyms. Define AGInt if it appears in public copy.

10. Design and accessibility requirements

Meet WCAG AA contrast for digital text and controls; gold is not automatically legible on every light or dark background.

Supply accessible document heading styles, tagged PDF exports, alt text, keyboard-friendly digital forms, and readable small text.

Provide color-independent meaning for status: label and icon plus color.

Test logo at favicon, profile avatar, letterhead, presentation, invoice, and embroidery/pin scale.

Preserve editable text in documents; avoid flattening core forms into images.

Supply RGB/sRGB for screens and CMYK proofed exports for print.

Include fonts or links and licenses; never package unlicensed font files.

Use real, approved legal entity, address, tax, banking, email, phone, and payment information only. Keep sensitive details out of public templates until an owner authorizes them.

11. Production and approval gates

Reference confirmation: Use the exact founder-approved badge/lockup source. If no clean vector exists, create a faithful vectorization for review; do not redesign.

Identity standards: Founder approves color sampling, final fonts, clear-space/minimum-size, and flat logo master.

Template architecture: Founder and department owners approve the document inventory and editable fields.

Legal/finance review: Counsel approves contract language; finance confirms legal entity, tax, invoice and payment fields.

Digital implementation: Web/brand owner checks contrast, responsive crops, metadata, and current website fit.

Production proof: Approve print proof and physical pin sample before manufacturing.

Release: Publish the source library with approved files, version numbers, and a concise usage guide.

15. Definition of done

The brand system is ready when AMA has one approved master mark; a documented, contrast-checked color and type system; usable identity variants; editable and export-ready files for each approved application; consistent department labeling; legal/finance-approved document fields; a physical production spec; and a concise governance guide that lets internal teams and AI tools extend the system without drifting from the approved identity.

North star: One recognizable AMA identity, carried consistently through every client and corporate touchpoint—from first brief to final invoice—while telling the truth about what the company can deliver today and what it is building toward.
